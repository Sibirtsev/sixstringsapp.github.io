What Is a Slash Chord?

A slash chord is a symbol like C/E: the chord is written on the left, and the bass note is written on the right. This notation quickly shows what should sound at the bottom.

June 17, 2026
Reader level: Arranger

In regular chord notation, we usually see only the chord name:

C
Am
G7
Fmaj7

But sometimes we need to specify which note should sound in the bass.

For example:

C/E

This means: play a C chord, but E should sound at the bottom.

This kind of notation is called a slash chord.

Simple definition

A slash chord is chord notation with a slash: the chord is written on the left, and the bass note is written on the right.

chord / bass

For example:

C/E

Read it like this:

C chord with E in the bass

More examples:

D/F♯ = D chord with F♯ in the bass
Am/G = Am chord with G in the bass
F/G  = F chord with G in the bass

The main idea is simple: the left side tells you which chord is sounding, and the right side tells you which note should be at the bottom.

How to read C/E

Let’s look at C/E.

On the left, we have C. This is a C major chord:

C = C–E–G

On the right, we have E. This is the bass note:

E = bass

So:

C/E = C major with E in the bass

You do not have to play the notes strictly in the order E–C–G. The important thing is that E is the lowest sounding note.

On guitar, this may be one fingering. In a band, the guitarist may play a regular C chord while the bassist plays E. The notation still means the same thing: E should sound at the bottom.

Slash chord and inversion: how they are connected

A slash chord can be an inversion, but it does not have to be one.

If the bass note belongs to the chord, it is an inversion.

For example, C major contains these notes:

C–E–G

In C/E, the bass note E belongs to the C chord. So C/E is the first inversion of C major.

In C/G, the bass note G also belongs to the C chord. So C/G is the second inversion.

But if we write:

C/D

D does not belong to the C chord:

C = C–E–G

So C/D is not an inversion. It is a slash chord with a separate bass note.

In short:

C/E = inversion, because E belongs to C
C/G = inversion, because G belongs to C
C/D = slash chord, because D does not belong to C

In What Is a Chord Inversion, we looked at inversions in detail. Here, remember the broader point: the slash shows the bass, not the chord type.

The easiest way to check a symbol is to spell the chord on the left and see whether the note on the right belongs to it.

Why slash chords are useful

Slash chords are not used to make notation more complicated. They solve a practical problem: they show bass motion.

They help you:

  • make the bass line smoother;
  • connect chords without large jumps;
  • create ascending or descending motion;
  • keep the upper chord similar while changing the foundation;
  • write an arrangement more precisely;
  • show what the bass or the left hand on piano should play;
  • make simple harmony more expressive.

For example, this progression:

C → F

sounds clear, but the bass jumps from C to F.

If we add C/E:

C → C/E → F

the bass moves more smoothly:

C → E → F

Here C/E is not used because we want a “complex” chord. It is used to lead the bass toward F.

Ascending bass line

One of the most common uses is a smooth rise in the bass.

For example:

C → C/E → F

Bass:

C → E → F

Another example:

G → G/B → C

Bass:

G → B → C

G/B is an inversion of G major because B belongs to G:

G = G–B–D

But in songs, the term is less important than the result: the bass leads the harmony smoothly into C.

Descending bass line

Slash chords are also often used for downward motion.

For example:

Am → Am/G → F

Bass:

A → G → F

Am/G is not an inversion of A minor, because G does not belong to Am:

Am = A–C–E

It is a slash chord with a separate bass note.

It sounds natural because it helps create a clear descending line.

Slash chord as a separate harmonic color

Sometimes a slash chord is used not only for smooth bass motion, but also for a special harmonic color.

For example:

F/G

On the left, we have F:

F = F–A–C

On the right, we have G:

G = bass

So we get:

F/G = G in the bass + F–A–C above it

This chord often feels like a soft dominant tension before C. It does not contain the usual third of a G chord, so it sounds softer than G7, but it still pulls toward C.

By ear, this is not just “F with an extra note”. The G in the bass changes the function of the whole structure.

How a slash chord differs from add, sus, and maj7

A slash chord does not describe a new chord type in the same way as sus, add, or maj7.

Compare:

Csus4 = C chord with the third replaced by the fourth
Cadd9 = C chord with an added ninth
Cmaj7 = C chord with a major seventh
C/E   = C chord with E in the bass

Csus4, Cadd9, and Cmaj7 tell you which notes belong to the chord itself.

C/E tells you which note should be in the bass.

These are different types of information.

How to play slash chords on guitar

On guitar, a slash chord is defined by the lowest sounding note.

If the symbol says:

C/E

E should sound below the other notes of the C chord.

If the symbol says:

D/F♯

F♯ should be in the bass.

You do not always need to play every note of the chord. Sometimes the bass plus a few important notes above it is enough.

For example, for C/E, it is important to hear:

E in the bass
C and G above it

The third, E, is already in the bass, so you do not have to double it on top. The main thing is that the chord is recognizable and the bass note is clear.

When another instrument plays the bass

In a band, the guitarist does not always have to play the bass note.

If the chart says:

C/E

it may mean:

guitar plays C
bass plays E

The overall sound is still C/E.

The same applies to piano: the right hand may play a C chord while the left hand plays E in the bass.

So a slash chord is not only a guitar fingering. It is a way to notate the overall harmony and bass distribution.

How to see a slash chord on the fretboard

Find the bass note first. Then add the chord.

For C/E:

  1. Find E on the lower strings.
  2. Build the notes of C nearby: C, E, G.
  3. Check that E sounds below everything else.
  4. Remove unnecessary doubled notes if the shape feels too heavy.

For Am/G:

  1. Find G in the bass.
  2. Add the notes of Am: A, C, E.
  3. Listen to how the G in the bass changes the feeling of Am.
  4. Compare Am and Am/G.
Am   = A–C–E
Am/G = G in the bass + A–C–E

The goal is not to find “the one correct fingering”. A single slash chord may have several useful shapes.

5-minute exercise

Build this progression:

C → C/E → F → G

First play only the chords. Then play only the bass:

C → E → F → G

After that, play everything together.

Listen not to the chord shape, but to the motion of the lowest voice. The main goal is to hear how C/E connects C and F.

Exercise with ascending and descending bass

Play two progressions:

C → C/E → F
Am → Am/G → F

In the first one, the bass rises:

C → E → F

In the second one, the bass descends:

A → G → F

Compare the feeling. An ascending bass often sounds like lift and increasing motion. A descending bass often sounds softer, calmer, or sadder.

Guitar exercise

  1. Take a regular C chord.
  2. Find a C/E shape.
  3. Find a C/G shape.
  4. Play C → C/E → C/G → C.
  5. Then play C → C/E → F.
  6. After that, try Am → Am/G → F.
  7. Make sure that the note on the right side of the slash is in the bass.

Do not measure this exercise by speed. The important thing is to hear the lowest note clearly.

Common mistakes

Thinking that every slash chord is an inversion. No. C/E is an inversion because E belongs to C. C/D is not an inversion because D does not belong to C.

Ignoring the right side of the symbol. In C/E, C is not enough. E must be in the bass. If E does not sound at the bottom, the meaning of the symbol is lost.

Looking for one correct fingering. A slash chord describes the sound, not one specific fretboard shape. There may be several possible shapes.

Playing too many notes. Sometimes the bass and the upper chord are enough, without unnecessary doubled notes.

Confusing slash chords with add or sus chords. Cadd9 tells you about an added note in the chord. C/E tells you about the bass. These are different things.

Not listening to the bass line. The main strength of slash chords is the motion of the lowest voice. If the bass is not heard as a line, the effect becomes weaker.

Short summary

A slash chord is notation in the form chord/bass. The chord is written on the left, and the note that should sound at the bottom is written on the right. If the bass note belongs to the chord, it is an inversion. If it does not, it is a separate slash chord with another bass note. In every case, the main thing is to listen to the bass: it often makes simple harmony smoother, more expressive, and more alive.
