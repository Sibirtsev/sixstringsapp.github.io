What Is a Seventh Chord?

A seventh chord is a four-note chord: a triad plus a seventh. It sounds richer than a basic triad and often creates a pull toward the next chord.

June 14, 2026
Reader level: Arranger

A triad gives a chord its foundation: root, third, and fifth. From these three notes, we can already hear the main quality of the chord: major, minor, diminished, or augmented.

But in many styles, three notes are often not enough. In blues, jazz, funk, soul, pop music, and modern guitar harmony, chords often sound denser and richer. One of the main ways to get that sound is to add a seventh to a triad.

That is how a seventh chord appears.

Simple definition

A seventh chord is a four-note chord: a triad plus a seventh.

General formula:

1–3–5–7

In simpler terms:

seventh chord = triad + seventh

For example, the C major triad C contains three notes:

C–E–G

If we add B, we get Cmaj7:

C–E–G–B

A seventh chord is not necessarily harder to hear. It simply has a fourth note that adds color and often creates motion toward the next chord.

What is the seventh?

The seventh is the seventh scale degree above the root of the chord.

From C, the seventh note is B:

C D E F G A B
1 2 3 4 5 6 7

In chords, the two most important sevenths to recognize are:

B  = major seventh above C
B♭ = minor seventh above C

If we add B to a C chord, we get Cmaj7:

C–E–G–B

If we add B♭, we get C7:

C–E–G–B♭

Both chords start from the same C–E–G triad. But the seventh is different, so the character is different: Cmaj7 sounds softer and wider, while C7 sounds tenser and more directional.

How a seventh chord differs from a triad

A triad often sounds like a clear harmonic point of support.

C = C–E–G

A seventh chord adds another shade to that support.

Cmaj7 = C–E–G–B
C7    = C–E–G–B♭

C sounds simple and stable. Cmaj7 sounds softer and deeper. C7 sounds more active: it often wants to move somewhere.

That is why seventh chords are used not only for a “prettier” sound. They help harmony move.

Why chords with 7 are not all the same

The number 7 in a chord symbol does not mean that all seventh chords are the same.

Compare four chords from C:

ChordFormulaNotesSimple feeling
Cmaj71–3–5–7C–E–G–Bsoft, wide, bright
C71–3–5–♭7C–E–G–B♭tense, directional
Cm71–♭3–5–♭7C–E♭–G–B♭minor, soft, dense
Cm7♭51–♭3–♭5–♭7C–E♭–G♭–B♭unstable, dark

You do not need to memorize all shapes as separate fingerings right away. It is more useful to see the logic:

first the triad, then the type of seventh, then possible changes to the fifth

Cmaj7: major triad plus major seventh

Cmaj7 is built from a C major triad and a major seventh.

Formula:

1–3–5–7

Notes:

C–E–G–B

Cmaj7 sounds soft, wide, and slightly dreamy. It is not as direct as a regular C triad, but it is also not as tense as C7.

This chord often appears in jazz, soul, pop, neo-soul, lo-fi, and calmer harmonic contexts.

C7: dominant seventh chord

C7 is built from a C major triad and a minor seventh.

Formula:

1–3–5–♭7

Notes:

C–E–G–B♭

C7 sounds tenser than Cmaj7. It often pulls toward F.

C7 → F

Why does this work? Inside C7 there are two important notes:

E  = third of the chord
B♭ = minor seventh of the chord

They want to resolve into notes of the F chord:

E  → F
B♭ → A

Because of this, C7 does not sound like a calm stopping point. It sounds like a chord with direction.

Cm7: minor triad plus minor seventh

Cm7 is built from a C minor triad and a minor seventh.

Formula:

1–♭3–5–♭7

Notes:

C–E♭–G–B♭

Cm7 sounds soft, minor, and denser than a regular minor triad. If a C minor triad gives the basic minor color, Cm7 makes it deeper and less plain.

This chord often appears in jazz, blues, funk, soul, pop music, and rock.

Cm7♭5: half-diminished seventh chord

Cm7♭5 is built from a diminished triad and a minor seventh.

Formula:

1–♭3–♭5–♭7

Notes:

C–E♭–G♭–B♭

You may see two common symbols:

Cm7♭5
Cø

This chord is often called a half-diminished seventh chord. It sounds unstable because it contains a diminished fifth.

At this stage, the main thing to remember is simple: this is no longer just a diminished triad. It is a four-note seventh chord.

Why seventh chords are useful

Seventh chords make harmony more expressive.

They help you:

  • make the sound richer;
  • create pull between chords;
  • show style more clearly;
  • add a blues, jazz, soul, or funk color;
  • reveal the function of a chord inside a key;
  • make a simple progression feel less flat.

For example, a basic progression may sound like this:

C – Am – F – G

If we replace the triads with seventh chords:

Cmaj7 – Am7 – Fmaj7 – G7

The harmony becomes softer, richer, and more directional. G7 is especially important: it pulls back to C more strongly than a regular G triad.

Seventh chord and function

Seventh chords often make the function of a chord easier to hear.

This is especially clear on the dominant.

In C major, G is the V chord:

G–B–D

If we add the minor seventh F, we get G7:

G–B–D–F

This chord pulls more strongly toward C:

G7 → C

Inside G7, two notes want to resolve:

B → C
F → E

That is why G7 sounds more directional than a regular G triad. The seventh does not merely decorate the chord — it helps you hear its function.

How to see a seventh chord on the fretboard

On guitar, seventh chords often require choices. The chord has four notes, while the guitar has six strings. You can double some notes, but you do not always need to.

For example, C7 contains:

C–E–G–B♭

The most important notes for the chord quality are often:

E  = third
B♭ = seventh

The third tells you whether the chord is major or minor. The seventh tells you the type of seventh chord and creates the pull.

The fifth can sometimes be omitted if it is not altered. The root may already be played by the bass or another instrument. That is why a guitarist does not always have to play all four notes in every shape.

A good part is not the one that includes every possible note. It is the one where the function is clear and there is still room for the other instruments.

5-minute exercise

  1. Play a C chord: C–E–G.
  2. Add B. You get Cmaj7.
  3. Replace B with B♭. You get C7.
  4. Play C7 → F.
  5. Return to C and compare C, Cmaj7, and C7.
C      = C–E–G
Cmaj7  = C–E–G–B
C7     = C–E–G–B♭

The main goal is to hear how the fourth note changes the character of the chord.

Exercise with different seventh chords

Build four chords from C:

Cmaj7  = C–E–G–B
C7     = C–E–G–B♭
Cm7    = C–E♭–G–B♭
Cm7♭5  = C–E♭–G♭–B♭

Play them slowly one after another.

Compare the sound:

  • Cmaj7 — soft and wide;
  • C7 — tense and directional;
  • Cm7 — minor and dense;
  • Cm7♭5 — unstable and dark.

Do not try to memorize all fingerings immediately. First hear the difference in character.

Guitar exercise

  1. Choose one seventh chord, for example C7.
  2. Find the root, third, fifth, and seventh inside it.
  3. Play only the third and seventh: E and B♭.
  4. Then add the root C.
  5. Then add the fifth G.
  6. Compare at which point the chord already becomes recognizable.

This helps you hear not only the shape, but also the function of each note inside the chord.

Common mistakes

Thinking that any chord with the number 7 is the same. Cmaj7, C7, Cm7, and Cm7♭5 are different chords. They have different formulas and different harmonic pull.

Confusing Cmaj7 and C7. Cmaj7 contains B. C7 contains B♭. These are different sevenths and different functions.

Looking only for a comfortable fingering. A comfortable shape is useful, but it is important to understand where the seventh is.

Always playing every note. In an ensemble, some notes may already be played by other instruments. Sometimes the third and seventh are enough.

Ignoring resolution. Many seventh chords become clearer when you hear where they want to move.

Treating the seventh as just decoration. The seventh does add color, but it also often changes the function of the chord.

Short summary

A seventh chord is a triad with an added seventh. The fourth note makes the chord richer, adds color, and often creates direction. To understand seventh chords, it is important to see not only the fingering, but also the intervals: the third, the fifth, and especially the seventh.
